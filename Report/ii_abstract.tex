Antarctic sea ice plays a central role in the uptake of heat and carbon dioxide by the Southern Ocean, and much of
this exchange takes place at the underside of the ice. The underside of pancake ice in the marginal ice zone has not
been reconstructed in three dimensions, as satellites and above-ice methods cannot observe it and sonar is too coarse
to resolve its centimetre-scale structure (\autoref{ch:review}). This project carried out a proof of concept for reconstructing an overhead
underwater surface with the stereo cameras of a BlueROV2 remotely operated vehicle.

A test rig, calibration board and set of targets were designed and built, and three datasets were recorded in a
swimming pool (\autoref{ch:design}). Plastic pyramid and egg carton tiles printed to a known geometry served as ground truth, while
painted foam blocks imitated features of the ice underside (\autoref{sec:ch4.targets}). Open-source methods were compared at each pipeline stage.

For calibration (\autoref{ch:calibration}), merging the greyscale and colour channel detections and tuning the detector raised the underwater
tag detection rate from 35 to 43\,\% (\autoref{subsec:ch5.optimising_detection}). Of five calibration approaches compared on repeated held-out splits, a
Brown-Conrady model fitted to underwater images gave a held-out reprojection error of 0.29 pixels, compared with
8.6 pixels for an in-air calibration, and measured tags on an independent target to within about 2\,mm (\autoref{subsec:ch5.results_analysis}).

Classical and learnt stereo matchers were then compared for depth (\autoref{ch:reconstruction}), along with four camera pose methods and three
ways of fusing frames into a surface. The learnt
FoundationStereo model gave the most complete depth maps and was the only matcher to keep the full ice shape in
direct sunlight (\autoref{sec:ch5.depth_test}). COLMAP gave the most accurate poses in shade, with an absolute trajectory error of 6.8\,mm (\autoref{subsec:ch5.pose_results}). The
final pipeline combines COLMAP poses, FoundationStereo depth and truncated signed distance function fusion, and
reconstructed the printed pyramids with median errors of about 2\,mm (\autoref{subsec:ch5.fusion_results}). The learnt model smoothed over partly hidden
curved surfaces, so reconstructions of real ice should be checked against a second method (\autoref{subsec:ch5.fusion_discussion}).

The project shows that the BlueROV2 cameras can reconstruct an overhead underwater surface with millimetre-scale
accuracy, and provides a dataset and pipeline as a foundation for deployment beneath sea ice (\autoref{ch:conclusions} and \autoref{ch:recommendations}).
