\subsection{Sizing for turbidity and range}
\label{subsec:ch4.boardsizing}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------
"Bio-optical sensors measuring light scattering and particulate backscatter record these temporary biological blooms as modest turbidity spikes in the range of ~1 to 3 NTU (Garcia et al., 2020; Schallenberg et al., 2019)"

Design around a turbidity of 3 NTU, choosing AprilTag for the worst case scenario.

The paper expresses detected marker size in pixels. At an FTU of 3.6, the detected marker size is as low as 21 pixels. In order to evaluate the capability of the marker systems to detect small markers in turbid underwater environments, the camera is centred in front of the markers and then moved further away, increasing the distance between the camera and the markers and therefore decreasing the marker size in pixels.

Underwater, the required pixel count rises above the 25-pixel rule: the minimum pixel size required for reliable detection increases significantly. The 25-pixel rule used in air is an absolute lower bound; underwater, aim for 40 to 60+ pixels per tag to maintain sub-pixel corner accuracy. We are going with a minimum of 40 pixels for the maximum turbidity situation.

We want to work with 1-2 metres calibrated, and the minimum focus distance is 25cm.

\textbf{Pin-hole model pixel calculation.} The pixel width $p$ of a tag in the image plane depends on the tag size $S$ (metres), distance $D$ (metres), and focal length $f_x$ (pixels):
\begin{equation}
p = \frac{S \cdot f_x}{D}.
\end{equation}
For a standard camera underwater (assuming a $60^\circ$ to $70^\circ$ horizontal field of view in water due to flat-port refraction), the focal length $f_x$ typically ranges between 1200 and 1400 pixels for a 1600-pixel wide sensor. Assuming an average focal length $f_x = 1300$ pixels:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Option A, $65\text{ mm}$ tag size ($S = 0.065\text{ m}$): at 1 m, $p \approx 84.5$ pixels (excellent detection); at 3 m, $p \approx 28.1$ pixels (slightly low for underwater contrast).
	\item Option B, $85\text{ mm}$ tag size ($S = 0.085\text{ m}$): at 1 m, $p \approx 110.5$ pixels (excellent detection); at 3 m, $p \approx 36.8$ pixels (safe margin for underwater blur).
\end{itemize}

The board finally used was chosen based on the commercially available option and the 50mm tag size indicated by the underwater testing:
\begin{verbatim}
family:       tag36h11
tag size:     0.150 m   (black square, outer edge)
plate size:   0.190 m   (adds 1-cell white quiet zone each side)
IDs:          0-3 (or however many targets are needed)
max_hamming:  0
resolution:   1600x1200
range:        0.3 - 2.0 m
\end{verbatim}

\subsection{Board material and manufacture}
\label{subsec:ch4.boardmaterial}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------
% DETERMINING BOARD SIZE AND MATERIAL
Dibond - give reference for why, comment on shrinkage.
Type of Dibond offered by Orms - give reference for which.
Size - give reference for why.

Do not use brushed aluminium: the aluminium shows through all white areas of the image, so the white squares are bare metal with low contrast under lights. This is bad for corner detection at an angle. Use a smooth white powder coat instead, since aluminium reflects differently at different angles.

A2 (420x695) is the sweet spot, being more manageable.

A custom quote is needed to avoid the wood sub-frame, which will swell and delaminate. Ask for a bare panel or drilled mounting holes.

Seal the cut edges. Dibond is two aluminium skins over a polyethylene core, and seawater might cause corrosion in the aluminium/PE crevice. A bead of epoxy will do the trick.

Ask about the gloss levels of the UV inks. Tend towards semi-gloss, and matte if possible. Ask about printer accuracy.

\section{Calibration stage}
\label{sec:ch4.calibrationstage}
%=====================================================================
\begin{enumerate}
	\item \texttt{build\_board()} builds a model of the board as a list of 3D points, in mm, of the corners of every tag.
	\item Detect the tags and refine the corners. For every image, the visible tags are detected and each tag corner is found to sub-pixel accuracy. This is the "observed" set.
\end{enumerate}

The settings for the detector stages described in Section~\ref{subsec:ch3.detectionstages} are stored in \texttt{detector\_presets.json} to allow for quick changing and testing.
