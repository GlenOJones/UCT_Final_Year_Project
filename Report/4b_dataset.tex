\chapter{Dataset}
\label{ch:dataset}

%=====================================================================
% SECTION: Test rig and Dataset
%=====================================================================
This project produced not only a report, but also an in-air and underwater
stereo dataset. The dataset contains calibration recordings of an AprilGrid
board, side scans of 3D-printed shapes with known CAD geometry, and
upward-looking scans of foam blocks that imitate the underside of sea ice.
This chapter first describes the calibration board and the sliding test rig used
to collect the data, then the reconstruction targets, and finally the three
datasets (DS1--DS3) and the conditions they were recorded in.
%==============
\section{Calibration Board and test Rig Construction}


% Calibration board
As explained in \autoref{subsec:ch3.targets}, an AprilGrid can still be used for
calibration when only some of its tags are detected. To get a target that is flat and
waterproof, a custom AprilGrid was generated with Kalibr
\cite{furgaleUnifiedTemporalSpatial2013, kalibrRepo} and printed on white aluminium
Dibond (\href{https://www.ormsprintroom.co.za/product/metal-prints/}{\textcolor{blue}{Orms Print Room}}, Cape Town). The board was sized as A1 with 50\,mm
tags, to match standard Kalibr targets \cite{calibioKalibrTargets}. Due to a
miscommunication, it was printed as A2 instead, which reduced the tag size to 35.36\,mm.
% Test rig

A custom sliding rig was built from 2020 aluminium extrusion. The ROV is
secured to 3D-printed rollers with ball bearings, which run along the
horizontal extrusions and let it slide towards and away from the calibration
board at the end of the rail. The board sits on an adjustable mount that can be
set from horizontal to vertical in 10\,$^\circ$ steps, and placed at the
left, centre or right of the end rail. This gives a repeatable distance, position
and angle between the board and the cameras across tests. The CAD files
for the sliding rail and calibration board image can be found within the project's github.

\section{3D printed targets}
\label{subsec:ch5.3d_printed_shapes}
The 3D-printed targets were designed by Agoritsa Spirakis for a UCT
undergraduate thesis \cite{aggi_thesis}. The set used here contains two pyramid
tiles and two sinusoidal ``egg carton'' tiles. Images of each shape are given in
\autoref{app:printed_shapes}, and the STL files are available in this project's
\href{https://github.com/GlenOJones/UCT_Final_Year_Project/tree/main/CAD/3D_prints}{\textcolor{blue}{GitHub repository}}.
\autoref{fig:ch5.printed_shapes} shows the four shapes, and \autoref{fig:ch4.data_side} shows them during data collection
(\autoref{subsec:ch5.recordings_tested}). Because their exact geometry is
known from the CAD files, the prints serve as ground truth. The reconstructed
point clouds are compared against them to measure accuracy.

\section{Fake Sea ice}
While the 3D prints are useful for measuring accuracy, they do not
represent sea ice well. The black surfaces differ from ice in both texture and contrast. 
Furthermore, under-ice scanning looks upwards towards the
surface, where incoming sunlight affects the images. To better approximate real ice, four foam structures were
cut with a hot wire and painted light blue. The set includes a grid of
2\,$\times$\,2\,cm cubes to emulate repetitive patterns, an overhang to emulate large
occlusions, a stepped pyramid to emulate layered ice, and a wave pattern to
emulate curved ice features.

\section{Datasets}
\label{sec:ch4.datasets}
Data was recorded both in air (ARU laboratory) and in water (swimming pool). The swiming pool recordings
were completed over three days, in varying lighting conditions. The weather, pool conditions and time 
of every recording used in this project are given in
\hyperref[app:recording_conditions]{Appendix~E}. 

All recordings were completed in 1600\,$\times$\,1200\,px, 30\,fps, MJPEG. The choice of MJPEG was to allow for more than 5 fps for 
continous capture aswell as due to an issue in setting the YUYV white balance when testing underwater. This is also the format supported by
the camera synchronisation that will likely be implemented on the system before a deployment.

The recordings used in this report are grouped into three datasets, DS1--DS3,
shown in \autoref{fig:ch4.datasets} and described below. Further recordings
were collected beyond these and are included in the full dataset.

\subsection{DS1: Calibration recordings}
\label{sec:ch4.ds1}
The calibration dataset was recorded on the test rail, with the calibration board mounted at one end.
The board was mounted at two positions across the rail, centre and left. Its angle, measured from the
horizontal, was set to 0, 20, 30, 40, 50 and 60\,$^\circ$ at the centre and 0, 20, 40 and 60\,$^\circ$ on the left.
At each angle, recordings were taken every 10\,cm at distances from 40 to 150\,cm.
This test was repeated both in air in the ARU experimantal space, and underwater in a students pool.

\subsection{DS2: Side scans of the 3D prints}
\label{sec:ch4.ds2}
The 3D-printed targets were fixed to a flat board with AprilTags placed around them. The tags provide a
ground-truth camera pose (\autoref{subsec:ch5.tag_poses}) and serve as reference points for separating the objects.
The ROV was rolled past the board, stopping every 10\,cm, and this was repeated with the stereo cameras
20, 30, 40, 50 and 60\,cm from the board.

\subsection{DS3: Upward-looking scans of the foam blocks}
\label{sec:ch4.ds3}
The four blocks (pyramid, overhang, wave and cubes) were held in place in the water while a swimmer
moved the ROV around below them, with the cameras facing directly upwards.
Each block was scanned separately, next to a flat foam block carrying AprilTags. As in DS2, the tags
provide a ground-truth pose reference. Each scan consists of a single pass. Because the ROV was not fixed
in place, the distance to the blocks was found by triangulating the AprilTags.

The scans were repeated in two conditions. The first set were in direct sunlight at around 13:00, and again in overcast
late-afternoon light at around 17:00. \autoref{fig:ch4.data_up} shows a scan of the pyramid in the
darker conditions.
