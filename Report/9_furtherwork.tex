\chapter{Recommendations}
\label{ch:recommendations}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------

Before the system is deployed beneath pancake ice, the following further work and research is recommended

\section{Testing on Full-Sized Ice Mock-ups}
The upward-looking scans of \hyperref[sec:ch4.ds3]{DS3} tested the system under varying light on surfaces
resembling ice, but the sharp block corners and textured background gave matching points that will not exist
beneath full-sized pancake ice. Where the whole field of view is filled with ice, the pipeline may fail, as visual
odometry did beneath freshwater ice in previous work discussed in \autoref{sec:ch2.imaging_challenges}. The system should
therefore be tested on full-sized mock-ups before a costly expedition to the \gls{miz}.


\section{Adding Texture and Contrast}
Several methods could give the matcher more to work with on uniform ice. Filtering such as \gls{clahe} could
raise the local texture, provided it does not shift pixel locations. Locking the exposure for the dark ice,
rather than auto exposure balancing ice and bright sky, may reveal more detail, although its effect on accuracy
at the ice edges must be checked. Projecting a pattern, such as a grid of dots, onto the ice would add texture
directly.


\section{Survey Planning and Synchronisation}
The effect of distance on accuracy was not examined. \hyperref[sec:ch4.ds2]{DS2} holds side-on scans at 20 to
60\,cm, and initial tests suggest the best accuracy at around 30\,cm, which should be confirmed along with the
fall-off away from the image centre. All tests were single passes, so survey patterns for full-sized ice should be
compared, and real-time \gls{vio} could automate the scans. The cameras should also be synchronised in hardware,
as the \gls{rov} will move faster than in these slow scans.


\section{Combining Sonar and Optical Data}
Multibeam sonar has mapped the underside of Antarctic sea ice over large areas at around 0.5\,m resolution
\cite{williamsThickDeformedAntarctic2015}, as discussed in \autoref{sec:ch2.acoustic_platforms}. This is too coarse for
pancake ice, but sonar does not depend on light, water clarity or texture. Turning the BlueROV2's scanning
sonar upwards would give the overall floe shape, while the stereo cameras add fine detail and the sonar fills
regions where matching fails.
