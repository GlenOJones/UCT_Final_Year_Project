\chapter{Recommendations}
\label{ch:recommendations}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------

This report examined the full reconstruction pipeline and tested it underwater. Several further tests and
features are recommended here as future work to be completed before the system is deployed.


\section{Testing on Full-Sized Ice Mock-ups}
The upward-looking ice scans of \hyperref[sec:ch4.ds3]{DS3} showed how the system responds to varying
lighting conditions and how well it reconstructs surfaces resembling ice, but they fall short of emulating a real
deployment. The sharp corners of the blocks and the texture in the background provided matching points that will
not be present beneath full-sized pancake ice. In a real scan, there will likely be regions where the whole field
of view is filled with ice, with no edges to match. This may cause the pipeline to fail, as was the case with visual odometry
beneath freshwater ice in previous under-ice work (\autoref{sec:ch2.imaging_challenges}). Before a costly and
time-consuming expedition to the \gls{miz}, the system should therefore be tested on full-sized mock-ups that more
closer resemble the underside of pancake ice.


\section{Filtering, Dehazing and Exposure Locking}
On full-sized ice, the feature detection and matching may fail. Filtering and dehazing could help, for example
\gls{clahe} to increase the local texture, provided it does not shift
the original pixel locations. Manually setting the camera exposure for the dark underside of the ice may also
improve results. Auto exposure adjusts to the whole scene, so it must cover both the dark ice and the bright sky,
which strains the cameras' dynamic range. Lowering the exposure may reveal more detail on the ice. The sky would
then be overexposed, but it holds no useful data. Further testing would be needed to confirm that the overexposed
sky does not reduce the accuracy at the edges of the ice.


\section{Distance Dependence and Optimal Range}
Due to time constraints, the effect of distance on accuracy was not examined in this report, although the optimal
distance from the ice is important for planning a deployment. \hyperref[sec:ch4.ds2]{DS2} contains the same
side-on scans at distances of 20 to 60\,cm from the targets. Initial testing showed the best accuracy at around
30\,cm, but further analysis is needed to confirm this. It would also be beneficial to measure how accuracy falls off
with distance from the centre of the field of view.


\section{Hardware Synchronisation}
The two StellarHD cameras are not synchronised in hardware. This was acceptable for the slow scans in this report,
but adapting the cameras for hardware synchronisation is advised before deployment, as the \gls{rov} will move faster under
its own propulsion.


\section{Survey Patterns and Automation}
All tests in this report were single passes. Full-sized pancake ice will likely need multiple passes for complete
coverage, so experimenting with the patterns followed beneath the ice could give better reconstructions.
Furthermore, real-time processing with \gls{vio} could allow automated scanning of
pancake ice, making scans faster and reducing time spent on deployment.


\section{Active Texture Projection}
Finally, active texture could be examined. Projecting a pattern, such as a grid of dots, onto the ice would add
texture to low-texture surfaces and help the stereo matching.
