\section*{Held-out evaluation data}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Held-out evaluation data}
\label{app:heldout}

This section sets out the data behind the held-out comparison of the calibration methods. It lists the
underwater recordings and frames that were used and the recordings that each of the 28 splits held out. It then
explains how the reprojection error of a held-out frame is scored, and how each board corner is assigned to an
image zone and a distance band. The comparison is run by \texttt{src/experiments/compensation/run\_comparison.py}
with the configuration \texttt{config\_full.json}, and the list of splits is saved with the results in
\texttt{run\_info.json}.

\subsection*{Underwater recordings and frames}
\phantomsection
\label{app:heldout:recordings}

The underwater calibration data are eight recordings of the Kalibr AprilGrid (AprilTag 36h11, 35.36\,mm tags,
$10\times7$), each a sweep of the board towards and away from the rig at one tilt. One stereo frame was extracted
per nominal distance, and the nominal distance in centimetres is the number in the file name
(\texttt{c1\_Left\_100.png} is the left image at a nominal 100\,cm). The corners were detected once by
\texttt{Calibration.py} with the \texttt{water\_final} detector preset on the gray channel. Only the images and
tags that run kept after its outlier rejection are used, so a camera can have fewer frames than its recording.
The measured distance is from the camera to the board centre, taken from each image's board pose in that
calibration.

\subsection*{Held-out splits}
\phantomsection
\label{app:heldout:splits}

Frames within one recording are near neighbours, so holding out single frames would test a model on almost the
data it was fitted to. Instead, whole recordings are held out, using every combination of two of the eight
recordings ($\binom{8}{2} = 28$ splits). In each split every technique is fitted on the six remaining recordings
and scored on all the kept frames of the two held-out ones. Every recording is therefore held out in 7 splits and
used for training in the other 21, and every held-out frame is scored by a model that never saw its recording.
All techniques are scored on exactly the same held-out corners in every split.

\subsubsection*{How a held-out frame is scored}
For a held-out image, the technique's intrinsics are fixed and only the board pose $(\mathbf{R},\mathbf{t})$ is
fitted, by minimising the pixel distance between the detected corners $\mathbf{u}_i$ and their projections
$\hat{\mathbf{u}}_i = \pi(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{X}_i + \mathbf{t})$, where $\mathbf{X}_i$ are the known board
coordinates and $\pi$ is the technique's camera model. The residual of corner $i$ is
$\mathbf{e}_i = \mathbf{u}_i - \hat{\mathbf{u}}_i$, and the held-out error of a split over a set $S$ of its
held-out corners is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{RMS}_S = \sqrt{\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S} \lVert \mathbf{e}_i \rVert^2 }.
\end{equation}
$S$ is all held-out corners for the overall figure, or only those in one image zone or distance band. The zones
are defined below, and the distance bands are near (200 to 800\,mm) and far (1000 to 1500\,mm). Each metric is
computed once per split, and the tables report the mean over the 28 splits with the 2.5 to 97.5 percentile range
of the 28 values. Because the pose is fitted to the corners it is scored on, part of any model error is absorbed
into it, so the held-out \gls{rms} is a lower bound on the error at an unknown pose.

\subsection*{Normalised radius and image zones}
\phantomsection
\label{app:heldout:zones}

Lens distortion and refraction errors grow with distance from the optical axis, so the residuals are also
reported by where the corner lies in the image. The reference point is the principal point $(c_x, c_y)$ of the
technique being scored, where the optical axis meets the image. The pixel distance of a corner at $(u, v)$ from
it is divided by $R_{\max}$, the distance from the principal point to the farthest of the four image corners,
\begin{equation}
  r = \frac{\sqrt{(u - c_x)^2 + (v - c_y)^2}}{R_{\max}}, \qquad
  R_{\max} = \max_{(u,\,v)\,\in\,\{0,\,1600\}\times\{0,\,1200\}} \sqrt{(u - c_x)^2 + (v - c_y)^2},
  \label{eq:app:radius}
\end{equation}
for the $1600 \times 1200$~px images. So $r = 0$ at the principal point and $r = 1$ at the farthest image corner.
Normalising by the image rather than using pixels gives the zone boundaries the same meaning for every technique,
whose principal points differ by a few pixels, and for any image resolution. The image is then split into three
concentric zones,
\begin{equation}
  \text{centre } r < 0.33, \qquad \text{mid } 0.33 \le r < 0.66, \qquad \text{outer } r \ge 0.66 .
\end{equation}

\autoref{tab:app:zones} gives the values for the underwater Brown-Conrady fit on all recordings. The other
techniques' principal points are within 4\,px of these, so their zone boundaries move by less than 4\,px.
